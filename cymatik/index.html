The Idea

Sound applies the forces.
The world decides what happens.

Many audio-reactive visualizers map analyzed audio directly onto predetermined visual parameters. CYMATIK is being built on a different model: audio and other control inputs apply forces to a persistent simulated environment, and the picture emerges from that world's behavior and evolving state.

Music supplies the forces. Physics determines the behavior. You explore the consequences.

ConventionalAudio → Visual parameter
CYMATIKAudio / control input → Force → Simulated world → Persistent state → Visual consequence

Not a passive visualizer. A responsive environment you play with.

Curiosity recommended

You don't need to be a musician. You don't need to be a visual artist. You don't need to understand the physics. You need curiosity and the desire to play.

Technical Foundation

One evolving technology stack.

CYMATIK is developed as a single stack. The working prototype establishes the real-time audiovisual instrument: audio analysis, interactive controls, and a visual-performance workflow. The Physics Lab research build develops the persistent, force-driven simulated-world architecture. Active R&D is bringing those capabilities together into the future CYMATIK instrument.

Working today

The instrument prototype

Shown in the demo above.

  • Real-time audio analysis. Live system audio, measured continuously.
  • Live GPU rendering. Interactive 3D visuals drawn in real time.
  • Control workflow. Parameter panels, presets, and recording of the visual output.
Research build

The Physics Lab

Working, but not shown in the demo.

  • Force-driven simulation. Audio applies impulses and pressure to a persistent simulated field whose state carries memory.
  • Live audio, GPU-rendered. Live system audio or audio files drive the world, drawn in real time.
  • MIDI control. Hardware controllers can be learned and mapped to parameters.
  • Recorded automation. Control moves can be recorded and replayed.
Active R&D

Bringing it together

Where the stack is heading.

  • One instrument. Carrying the force-driven world into the same instrument and control surface shown in the demo.
  • Designed worlds and materials. Expanding the range of simulated environments and how they look and behave.
  • Control architecture. Making controls map cleanly to forces and world behavior across different worlds.
  • Presentation surfaces. Keeping a composition intact across screen shapes and projection canvases.
Future direction

Not yet built. OSC and other multimodal control alongside MIDI. A Windows standalone application and a VST3 workflow for Ableton Live. Commercial release, with licensing, distribution, and additional scenes, announced when the instrument is ready.

Questions

CYMATIK FAQ

Is CYMATIK a finished product?

Not yet. It is a working prototype in active development. The demo is real software being played, while the Physics Lab research build develops the force-driven world architecture. Bringing them together into one instrument is ongoing work.

When will CYMATIK be available?

Lotus Labz will announce availability when the instrument is ready. Until then this page is the official preview.

What platforms and hosts will be supported?

Development is on Windows. A standalone application and a VST3 workflow for Ableton Live are planned directions, not yet delivered. Final compatibility will be confirmed before launch.

Is the demo real?

Yes. It was recorded from the running software, with the audio you hear driving the visuals.

Do I need to understand physics?

No. The physics is the engine, not the interface. You only need curiosity.

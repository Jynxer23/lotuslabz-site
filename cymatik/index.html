Create

Build a world.
Let the music disturb it.

CYMATIK: Physics Lab is a real-time audiovisual instrument. Choose a world, decide what it is made of, and play any audio through it. Every hit leaves a mark that spreads, meets the next one and fades on its own.

Music supplies the forces. Physics determines the behavior. You explore the consequences.

Sound in

Play anything on your PC. CYMATIK listens to Windows system audio, or you can load an audio file.

Worlds

A surface, a tunnel and experimental twin-surface worlds. Each one is a persistent space that remembers what struck it.

Materials

Dots, membrane, clay and metal. Change material in the middle of a ripple and the wave carries on.

Camera and control

Four camera shots, with live control of force, physics, appearance and color.

Go deeper: the five systems behind every world

Every control belongs to one of five systems. Together they follow the path from the force that enters the world to the color you finally see.

  1. 01

    EXCITATION

    How energy enters the world.

    The forces and impulses that drive the simulation. Choose where they land, how they are shaped, and how hard they hit.

    • Placement
    • Point or rim force
    • Transient force
    • Sustained force
  2. 02

    PHYSICS

    How the world responds.

    Propagation, dispersion, damping and domain. The same strike can ring clean, smear into shimmer, or die away.

    • Dispersion
    • Damping
    • Spatial detail
    • Domain
  3. 03

    STRUCTURE

    What the world is made from.

    Experimental

    Place a physical structure inside the world. It has its own physics and its own memory, and the same events strike it.

    • Sphere
    • Size and gap
    • Strike patterns
    • Own material
  4. 04

    APPEARANCE

    How the world is rendered.

    Point surface, camera and depth. Stand inside the world, move through it, and decide how much of it you see.

    • Point density
    • Height scale
    • Camera
    • Haze
    • CHRMZ optics
  5. 05

    COLOR

    How the world gets its visual identity.

    Live control of hue, automatic color motion, and the lit room that reflective materials mirror.

    • Hue spectrum
    • Auto color
    • Room (OPTX)

Appearance and color change how the world is seen. They never change its physics, and nothing in them can erase the world's state.

Go deeper: circumferential physics and CHRMZ optics Experimental
Circumferential PhysicsExperimental

Physics around the circumference.

Most audio visuals turn loudness into a visual parameter. CYMATIK can instead let sound strike a world whose circumference is part of the physics. In the TNLZ tunnel world, three controls decide how that circle behaves.

TOPOLOGYWhat the circle is. One continuous fabric, or a ring of independent panels, up to 128.
EXCITATIONHow many places an event strikes at once, up to 128.
POLARITYWhich way each strike pushes: out, in, or alternating.

Dispersion and damping then decide how each strike travels and fades. The world keeps its state, so every strike leaves a consequence that spreads, interferes with the next one, and dies away on its own.

CHRMZ OPTICS

Experimental

Real-time reflective-material research. CHRMZ asks whether worlds and structures can reflect one another, and the room around them, while the physics keeps moving.

Experimental. Reflection behavior is still being refined, and CHRMZ is not a finished material.

Perform

Hands on the music.
Eyes on the room.

Every control stays live while the world runs. Steer it from your hardware, recall a whole setup from a preset slot, record your own moves, and send a clean picture to the room.

LIVE CONTROL

Every parameter stays playable during the performance. Move a slider and the world answers while it is still moving.

MIDI LEARN

Touch a CYMATIK control, move a knob on your controller, and the two are paired. Maps can be saved and recalled.

PRESETS

Twelve slots hold the whole setup: world, material, force shape, physics, camera and appearance. Name them and recall them.

AUTOM8

Record how you move the controls, then play the motion back, pause it or loop it. AUTOM8 keeps your control moves, not picture or sound. For video, see RenderLab below.

The audience sees the world. The artist sees the instrument.

Produce · RenderLab

Turn a performance
into a video.

From sound to social. From performance to production.

RenderLab records your performance, then renders it again offline as a clean video master. You choose the shape afterwards, so one performance can become a widescreen video for your site and a portrait video for Reels, Shorts or TikTok.

  1. Perform and record

    Play your set and press record. RenderLab keeps what the world did and the audio Windows was playing. A quick preview video is ready seconds after you stop.

  2. Choose the shape and settings

    Pick widescreen, ultrawide, square, 4:5 or 9:16, a size and a frame rate. Enter your own size if you need one.

  3. Render the video master

    RenderLab rebuilds the performance frame by frame, offline, and writes an MP4 with a lossless audio file beside it.

  4. Finish and share

    Open the MP4 in your video editor to trim, add captions or titles, and upload it to your site or your social platforms.

Native portrait, with exceptions

For 9:16, 4:5 and 1:1, RenderLab moves CYMATIK's camera to fit the new shape instead of stretching the picture. A vertical FLATZ world, and narrow shapes from takes that use Event Horizon or OZL8, fall back to a letterboxed or center-cropped frame. For the best result, record in a window shaped like your finished video.

What to know

  • RenderLab does not trim, caption or publish. Finish in your video editor.
  • It records the audio Windows plays, not a microphone.
  • Rendering runs slower than the performance, so plan for it.
  • Tested on Windows 11 with an NVIDIA graphics card. Rendering needs Chrome or Edge installed. Other setups are not yet verified.
  • Very wide masters may need a video extension from the Microsoft Store to play in Windows.

RenderLab is in private beta and is planned for CYMATIK 1.0.

Questions

CYMATIK FAQ

Is CYMATIK a finished product?

Not yet. The Windows build of CYMATIK: Physics Lab is in private beta, and commercial release is coming soon. Experimental features are marked on this page.

Can I buy it or try it now?

Not yet. Nothing can be purchased or downloaded today. A 14-day trial is planned, along with a $149 perpetual license. The planned introductory price is $99 for the first 30 days after launch. Lotus Labz will announce availability when the release is ready.

Can RenderLab make videos for Reels, Shorts and TikTok?

RenderLab can render 9:16 portrait video, with the exceptions listed in the RenderLab section. It does not trim, caption or post. Open the MP4 in your video editor to finish it and upload it to the platform you use.

Does CYMATIK work with Resolume or other VJ software?

Windows Spout output is in development. It is not released, and compatibility with specific VJ software has not been confirmed yet. Lotus Labz will say so here once it is.

What do I need to run it?

Windows 11 and an NVIDIA graphics card are recommended, based on current testing. Final system requirements are pending validation, and no performance guarantees are published yet.

What platforms will be supported?

CYMATIK is being built for Windows. Other platforms and plugin formats are not announced.

Is the demo real?

Yes. It was recorded from the running software, with the audio you hear driving the visuals. It is an earlier prototype capture, and updated footage is planned.

Do I need to understand physics?

No. The physics is the engine, not the interface. You only need curiosity.

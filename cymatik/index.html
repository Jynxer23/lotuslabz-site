The Idea

Sound applies the forces.
The world decides what happens.

Many audio-reactive visualizers map analyzed audio directly onto predetermined visual parameters. CYMATIK is being built on a different model: audio and other control inputs apply forces to a persistent simulated environment, and the picture emerges from that world's behavior and evolving state.

Music supplies the forces. Physics determines the behavior. You explore the consequences.

ConventionalAudio → Visual parameter
CYMATIKAudio / control input → Force → Simulated world → Persistent state → Visual consequence

Not a passive visualizer. A responsive environment you play with.

Curiosity recommended

You don't need to be a musician. You don't need to be a visual artist. You don't need to understand the physics. You need curiosity and the desire to play.

Inside the Instrument

Five systems.
One responsive world.

Every control in CYMATIK belongs to one of five systems. Together they follow the path from the force that enters the world to the color you finally see.

This section and the ones after it describe the current CYMATIK build, the Physics Lab. It is working software, and it is not what the demo video above shows.

  1. 01

    EXCITATION

    How energy enters the world.

    The forces and impulses that drive the simulation. Choose where they land, how they are shaped, and how hard they hit.

    • Placement
    • Point or rim force
    • Transient force
    • Sustained force
  2. 02

    PHYSICS

    How the world responds.

    Propagation, dispersion, damping and domain. The same strike can ring clean, smear into shimmer, or die away.

    • Dispersion
    • Damping
    • Spatial detail
    • Domain
  3. 03

    STRUCTURE

    What the world is made from.

    Experimental

    Place a physical structure inside the world. It has its own physics and its own memory, and the same events strike it.

    • Sphere
    • Size and gap
    • Strike patterns
    • Own material
  4. 04

    APPEARANCE

    How the world is rendered.

    Point surface, camera and depth. Stand inside the world, move through it, and decide how much of it you see.

    • Point density
    • Height scale
    • Camera
    • Haze
    • CHRMZ optics
  5. 05

    COLOR

    How the world gets its visual identity.

    Live control of hue, automatic color motion, and the lit room that reflective materials mirror.

    • Hue spectrum
    • Auto color
    • Room (OPTX)

Appearance and color change how the world is seen. They never change its physics, and nothing in them can erase the world's state.

Physical Worlds

Choose the world.
Choose what it is made of.

The physics and the picture are separate. One persistent world can be read as points, a membrane, clay or metal, and switching material in the middle of a ripple does not disturb the wave.

Worlds and materials are still evolving, and more configurations are in development. Experimental ones are marked, and none of them should be read as final.

Circumferential PhysicsExperimental

Physics around the circumference.

Most audio visuals turn loudness into a visual parameter. CYMATIK can instead let sound strike a world whose circumference is part of the physics. In the TNLZ tunnel world, three controls decide how that circle behaves.

TOPOLOGYWhat the circle is. One continuous fabric, or a ring of independent panels, up to 128.
EXCITATIONHow many places an event strikes at once, up to 128.
POLARITYWhich way each strike pushes: out, in, or alternating.

Dispersion and damping then decide how each strike travels and fades. The world keeps its state, so every strike leaves a consequence that spreads, interferes with the next one, and dies away on its own.

CHRMZ OPTICS

Experimental

Real-time reflective-material research. CHRMZ asks whether worlds and structures can reflect one another, and the room around them, while the physics keeps moving.

Experimental. Reflection behavior is still being refined, and CHRMZ is not a finished material.

Play It Like an Instrument

Touch it. Learn it.
Record it. Play it back.

Every control stays live while the world runs. Pair it with hardware, recall a whole setup, automate it, and keep the performance.

LIVE CONTROL

Every parameter stays playable during the performance. Move a slider and the world answers while it is still moving.

MIDI CONTROL

Hardware controllers can be learned. Touch a CYMATIK control, move a knob, and the two are paired. Maps can be saved and recalled.

PRESETS

Twelve slots hold the whole instrument: world, material, force shape, physics, camera and appearance. Name them and recall them. A preset saves the setup, not the wave that happens to be moving.

AUTOM8

Record how you move the controls, then play the motion back, pause it, or loop it. Keep the best moves as automation clips.

PERFORMANCE RECORDING

A whole performance of control moves, from its opening state to its last gesture, can be saved by name and recalled. Playback starts from the recorded opening state.

Performance Output

The audience sees the world.The artist sees the instrument.

OperatorControls + world
AudienceWorld only

CYMATIK keeps the control interface with the performer and sends a clean surface to the audience. Open Output creates a second window that carries the world and nothing else: no header, no tabs, no cards, no read-outs. Place it on a projector or display and double-click for full screen.

It is a second eye on the same world, not a second world. One state, one clock, and each surface frames the world for its own shape, so the composition holds on 16:9, ultrawide and projector canvases.

Bringing it together

Where the stack is heading.

  • One instrument. Carrying the force-driven world into the same instrument and control surface shown in the demo.
  • Designed worlds and materials. Expanding the range of simulated environments and how they look and behave.
  • Control architecture. Making controls map cleanly to forces and world behavior across different worlds.
  • Presentation surfaces. Keeping a composition intact across screen shapes and projection canvases.

Integration of the current instrument and Physics Lab research into a unified CYMATIK experience, with OSC and additional control inputs alongside MIDI, Windows standalone and VST3 workflows, and eventual commercial distribution.

Questions

CYMATIK FAQ

Is CYMATIK a finished product?

Not yet. It is a working prototype in active development. The demo is real software being played, while the Physics Lab research build develops the force-driven world architecture. Bringing them together into one instrument is ongoing work.

When will CYMATIK be available?

Lotus Labz will announce availability when the instrument is ready. Until then this page is the official preview.

What platforms and hosts will be supported?

Development is on Windows. A standalone application and a VST3 workflow for Ableton Live are planned directions, not yet delivered. Final compatibility will be confirmed before launch.

Is the demo real?

Yes. It was recorded from the running software, with the audio you hear driving the visuals.

Do I need to understand physics?

No. The physics is the engine, not the interface. You only need curiosity.
